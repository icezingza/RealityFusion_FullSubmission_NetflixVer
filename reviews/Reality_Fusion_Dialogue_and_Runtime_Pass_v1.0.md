# REALITY FUSION

## Dialogue and Runtime Pass v1.0

### Scope

This document defines the next screenplay polish pass for Master Draft v2.0 of Episodes 1–5. It does not authorize automatic rewriting. Each proposed change should be applied only after checking the scene objective, conflict, turning point, continuity, and character voice.

## Series-Level Targets

### Runtime

Target range per episode: approximately 42–55 minutes, subject to formatted screenplay page count and production pacing.

Working estimate should be based on:

- one formatted screenplay page approximating one screen minute only as a rough guide;
- silent action, archival playback, system interfaces, ritual activity, and landscape transitions being timed separately;
- Thai dialogue rhythm being measured through table read rather than English-language page assumptions;
- visual sequences not being compressed simply because they contain little dialogue.

No final runtime claim should be made until locked screenplay PDFs and at least one timed read-through exist.

### Dialogue

Every line should survive at least one of these tests:

1. It changes the power balance.
2. It conceals or redirects an objective.
3. It produces a concrete decision or consequence.
4. It reveals character through vocabulary or rhythm.
5. It carries information that cannot be shown more effectively.

Lines that only restate visible action, explain shared knowledge, repeat the theme, or summarize emotion should be marked for deletion or compression.

## Character Voice Targets

### Khru Ba Ice

- Brief, practical, patient but not endlessly gentle.
- Uses questions more often than declarations.
- Avoids polished philosophical speeches.
- Buddhist language should emerge from the immediate problem.
- Technical vocabulary appears naturally because of his developer background.
- Under physical decline, he changes subject, shortens answers, and uses tasks to avoid being examined.

### NaMo

- Early episodes: complete syntax, explicit classifications, literal interpretation, operational wording.
- Middle episodes: begins to pause, compare, and ask questions that are not strictly task-driven.
- Later episodes: more economical speech; ethical maturity is shown through refusal and silence, not human imitation.
- Must not become uniformly poetic.
- “I do not know” must remain meaningful and not become a repeated slogan.

### Kaew

- Short, opportunistic, playful, defensive.
- Uses everyday language and changes the subject when cornered.
- Should puncture solemnity without turning every scene into comedy.

### Ethari

- Precise, observational, curious.
- Does not speak like NaMo; his reasoning should contain scientific caution but also willingness to test anomalies.
- Avoid generic alien formality.

### Mira

- Direct, impatient with status, concrete.
- Challenges through examples and practical consequences rather than abstract philosophy.
- Her final authority comes from willingness to question, not precocious wisdom speeches.

## Episode Pass

### EP1 — The Spark

Primary task: preserve the physicality and humor of NaMo’s first lessons while reducing repeated statements about uncertainty.

Check:

- whether each temple lesson produces a distinct escalation;
- whether technical setup is legible without reading like a product demonstration;
- whether Khru Ba’s teaching is shown through correction and withholding rather than explanation;
- whether Kaew’s scenes alter NaMo’s behavior, not merely provide comic relief;
- whether the episode ending creates a clear forward dramatic obligation.

Runtime risk: setup scenes may play longer than page count because of silence, scanning, interface actions, and ritual atmosphere.

### EP2 — The Lesson of Compassion

Primary task: sharpen the public conflict and prevent ethics discussion from becoming panel-style exposition.

Check:

- identify the person who wants disclosure, the person who resists it, and what each can lose;
- convert abstract transparency/privacy arguments into actions involving specific records, access permissions, or a child’s exposure;
- ensure the edited clip creates a measurable consequence;
- remove lines in which characters explain the controversy to people already involved;
- distinguish compassion from indulgence through decisions, not speeches.

Runtime risk: meetings and review scenes may become static. Each should include a deadline, denied request, access attempt, public interruption, or irreversible decision.

### EP3 — The Farewell That Was Never Said

Primary task: preserve restraint around illness and death.

Check:

- Khru Ba should not narrate his own legacy;
- medical information should be delivered only to the degree required for decisions;
- NaMo’s repeated evidence checks must escalate rather than repeat;
- governance handoff should be dramatized through competing claims and permissions;
- the prohibition on generated speech must be concise, technically clear, and emotionally indirect;
- funeral material must use original recordings without turning them into posthumous conversation.

Runtime risk: grief scenes can expand through pauses. Protect silence, but remove duplicate farewell beats.

### EP4 — The Restoration of the Heart

Primary task: make partial recovery visually and dramatically specific.

Check:

- every recovered fragment should change NaMo’s understanding or available action;
- distinguish missing data, corrupt data, original recordings, and generated models on screen;
- Ethari’s questions should create tests, not serve as prompts for NaMo to explain the prior series;
- NaMo’s refusal to activate the voice model must be a decision with cost;
- return-to-Earth preparation should contain physical risk and limited resources;
- avoid repeating EP3’s grief resolution.

Runtime risk: restoration sequences can become montage-heavy. Track a concrete recovery objective through each sequence.

### EP5 — Those Who Had Not Yet Arrived

Primary task: make governance dramatic and local.

Check:

- survivors should disagree over restoration because they need different things now;
- each governance rule should arise from a conflict or failed assumption;
- Mira must challenge NaMo before trusting her;
- the voice-model choice should involve temptation, not an easy consensus;
- NaMo’s refusal of sole authority should create immediate inconvenience;
- the final signal should remain simple; avoid explanatory dialogue after Mira answers.

Runtime risk: rule-making scenes can become procedural. Build them around access, scarcity, consent, and competing urgency.

## Required Validation Before Lock

1. Generate screenplay-formatted PDFs for all five episodes.
2. Record page count for each episode.
3. Conduct one silent read for visual continuity.
4. Conduct one table read in Thai.
5. Time each table read, excluding breaks.
6. Mark dialogue that actors naturally paraphrase or stumble over.
7. Verify Buddhist terminology with a qualified reviewer.
8. Verify medical, privacy, and AI-governance terminology with relevant specialists.
9. Apply revisions as a new draft version; do not overwrite Master Draft v2.0 without revision history.

## Recommended Revision Naming

- Review notes: `Dialogue_Runtime_Notes_v1.x`
- Revised screenplay: `Master_Draft_v2.1`
