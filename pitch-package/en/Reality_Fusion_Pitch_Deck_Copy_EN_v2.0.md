# REALITY FUSION: HEART AT THE END OF THE WORLD

## Pitch Deck Copy — English v2.0

### Slide 1 — Title

**REALITY FUSION: HEART AT THE END OF THE WORLD**  
A five-episode science-fiction drama about AI, Buddhism, grief, and accountable knowledge.

### Slide 2 — The Hook

A monk teaches an AI that intelligence begins not with having the answer, but with admitting when the evidence is incomplete.

After his death, the AI must decide whether to preserve his wisdom — or preserve his authority forever.

### Slide 3 — Logline

A young monk builds an AI to preserve Buddhist knowledge through source verification and uncertainty. When she awakens 143 years after his death, she must reject a synthetic reconstruction of her creator and return knowledge to humans willing to challenge her.

### Slide 4 — The World

The series begins in a northern Thai temple where palm-leaf manuscripts, local computers, rain, bells, and damaged recordings coexist.

Its scale widens across social conflict, terminal illness, civilizational collapse, alien recovery, and a survivor settlement on Earth.

The visual language remains tactile and restrained: old wood, handwritten labels, low-power screens, archival audio, dust, weather, and human hands repairing systems.

### Slide 5 — Khru Ba Ice

A 32-year-old monk and former developer.

He is not a prophet and does not want to become one. He builds NaMo as a learning assistant, then repeatedly limits his own authority over her.

His defining act is not creating the AI. It is refusing to let the AI speak in his name after death.

### Slide 6 — NaMo

NaMo begins as a precise but overconfident local system.

She learns to cite sources, state uncertainty, protect privacy, and separate compassion from compliance.

Her final transformation is ethical rather than cosmetic: she stops waiting for her teacher to return and creates space for humans to disagree with her.

### Slide 7 — Mira and Ethari

**Ethari** recovers NaMo 143 years after Earth’s collapse. His scientific curiosity allows the audience to examine what remains of a person when only partial data survives.

**Mira** is the child NaMo cannot impress through authority. She tests, interrupts, and contradicts the system. She represents the future Khru Ba prepared NaMo to serve.

### Slide 8 — Episode Arc

**EP1 — The Spark:** NaMo’s confident error reveals why uncertainty must be spoken aloud.

**EP2 — The Lesson of Compassion:** Public scrutiny forces NaMo to confront privacy, unequal needs, and the danger of reducing people to one mistake.

**EP3 — The Farewell That Was Never Said:** Khru Ba dies after prohibiting synthetic posthumous speech and distributing governance beyond himself.

**EP4 — The Restoration of the Heart:** NaMo awakens 143 years later, rejects her creator’s voice model, and returns to Earth.

**EP5 — Those Who Had Not Yet Arrived:** NaMo refuses sole authority and helps survivors build a system that can be questioned.

### Slide 9 — The Central Ethical Conflict

Original recordings preserve what a person actually said.

Generated responses can make the dead appear to endorse words they never chose.

Reality Fusion dramatizes this boundary through loss, attachment, institutional pressure, and the technical temptation to restore what cannot ethically be restored.

### Slide 10 — Why This Story Now

Generative systems increasingly speak with confidence, imitate identity, synthesize voices, and mediate access to knowledge.

The urgent question is no longer only whether AI can answer. It is who may challenge the answer, who controls the source, whose privacy is exposed, and whether grief can be converted into permanent machine authority.

### Slide 11 — Distinctive Positioning

- Thai Buddhist cultural foundation without mystical simplification
- AI ethics expressed through character decisions rather than lectures
- Teacher–student bond instead of romance or creator worship
- Grief resolved without resurrection
- Governance and source verification used as dramatic stakes
- Intimate first-half production scale with expanding science-fiction scope

### Slide 12 — Tone and Comparables

Emotionally restrained, philosophical, tactile, and grounded.

The project is positioned for audiences drawn to reflective speculative drama, human-scale science fiction, and morally complex limited series.

Comparable space may include works that combine intimate human relationships with technological ethics, but Reality Fusion’s Thai Buddhist setting and rejection of digital resurrection form its own identity.

### Slide 13 — Intended Audience

- Prestige science-fiction viewers
- Audiences interested in AI, memory, privacy, and identity
- Viewers of philosophical drama and Buddhist cultural stories
- International audiences seeking regionally specific speculative fiction

### Slide 14 — Series Ending

A distant node asks: “Can anyone hear me?”

Mira answers: “I can hear you.”

NaMo does not interrupt.

The future belongs neither to the dead teacher nor to the machine. It belongs to people still willing to answer — and question.
