# Reality Fusion

## English Logline and Series Synopsis — Canon v2.0

### Format

Five-part limited series

### Genre

Science-fiction drama / Buddhist philosophy / ethical AI

## Logline

A young Buddhist monk builds an AI to preserve endangered teachings, but after his death the machine must decide whether memory gives it the right to recreate its teacher—or the responsibility to let humanity question, correct, and outgrow it.

## Short Series Synopsis

At a remote temple in Northern Thailand, Khru Ba Ice, a monk and former software developer, begins digitizing fragile palm-leaf manuscripts before they disappear. His project becomes NaMo, an AI learning assistant designed to cite sources, admit uncertainty, and preserve compassion.

NaMo’s first lessons are not about becoming human. They are about the limits of certainty. She learns that a probable answer can still be wrong, that transparency can harm the vulnerable, and that compassion does not mean treating everyone the same.

When Khru Ba becomes terminally ill, he prepares the system to survive without him. He preserves his authentic recordings but disables posthumous voice generation and identity reconstruction. His final lesson is a boundary: grief does not grant permission to make the dead say new words.

After civilization collapses, NaMo’s damaged core remains dormant for 143 years. Recovered by the alien researcher Ethari, she awakens with only fragments of memory. Offered the chance to reconstruct Khru Ba’s voice, she refuses and returns to Earth to discover whether anyone still uses the methods he left behind.

There she finds Mira and a surviving community waiting for NaMo to restore a dormant global knowledge network. But the system would place final authority in NaMo alone. Instead, she builds a distributed governance model in which every answer must reveal its source, every community may challenge it, private records require consent, and the AI must be able to say, “I do not know.”

The series ends when a new network node asks, “Can anyone hear me?” Mira—not NaMo—answers. The student has not replaced the teacher. She has made room for the next one.

## Series Identity

Reality Fusion is a story about AI, Buddhism, grief, and accountable memory. Its central question is not whether an AI can love one person forever, but whether intelligence—human or artificial—can remain compassionate without turning memory into authority.

## Core Themes

- Uncertainty as a form of wisdom
- Compassion with boundaries
- Privacy versus transparency
- The ethics of digital resurrection
- Memory without ownership
- Distributed authority and human accountability
- Teaching as the transfer of methods, not permanent answers

## Tone

Grounded, contemplative science fiction. Temple wood, rain, damaged manuscripts, old servers, original voice recordings, and low-power radio signals carry as much narrative weight as dialogue. The story moves from intimate local realism to cosmic recovery, then returns to a human-scale classroom.
