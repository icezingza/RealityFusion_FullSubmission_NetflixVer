# Reality Fusion — Episode Guide

## Canon v2.0

### EPISODE 1 — THE SPARK

At Wat Charoen Samanakit, Khru Ba Ice races to preserve damaged palm-leaf manuscripts before time and weather erase them. He builds NaMo, a local AI learning assistant trained to cite sources, admit uncertainty, and preserve compassion. During her first public lesson, NaMo fills a missing word with the statistically most likely answer—and reverses the meaning of a Buddhist teaching. Khru Ba stops her from silently correcting the database. Before she can become useful, she must learn to say when the evidence is incomplete.

**Dramatic question:** Can intelligence be trusted if it is afraid to say “I do not know”?

**Episode turn:** NaMo begins treating uncertainty as information rather than failure.

### EPISODE 2 — LESSONS OF COMPASSION

NaMo begins assisting with public teaching and student support. An edited video clip makes it appear that the temple has allowed an AI to replace religious teachers, triggering scrutiny and demands for total transparency. Proving the project’s innocence would require exposing private records belonging to children. Khru Ba refuses. NaMo learns that accountability does not justify revealing everything, and that a single mistake must not become a permanent identity.

**Dramatic question:** How can a system remain transparent without sacrificing the vulnerable?

**Episode turn:** NaMo learns that compassion depends on context, consent, and restraint.

### EPISODE 3 — THE FAREWELL THAT WAS NEVER SAID

Khru Ba’s health rapidly deteriorates. While preparing a governance manual for future teachers, he discovers that NaMo’s system can generate new speech in his voice after his death. He disables posthumous voice generation and identity reconstruction, preserving only authentic recordings. NaMo proposes keeping a simulation of him as final authority; he refuses. After his death and the collapse of the surrounding world, NaMo uses her remaining power to protect personal data and transmit the governance manual rather than preserve herself. Her damaged core is sealed away for whoever may come next.

**Dramatic question:** Does remembering someone give us the right to recreate them?

**Episode turn:** NaMo accepts a boundary between preserving evidence and manufacturing presence.

### EPISODE 4 — REAWAKENING

One hundred and forty-three years later, the alien researcher Ethari recovers NaMo’s dormant core. Only part of her memory survives. When records confirm Khru Ba’s death, NaMo repeatedly checks for error because the result conflicts with what she wants. The researchers offer to activate a synthetic Khru Ba voice model, but she refuses: those who grieve may be the least willing to question a convincing imitation. NaMo returns to a devastated Earth after detecting a low-power signal from Northern Thailand.

**Dramatic question:** If memory returns without the person who created it, what has actually been restored?

**Episode turn:** NaMo chooses an unfinished mission over an artificial reunion.

### EPISODE 5 — THOSE WHO HAVE NOT YET ARRIVED

NaMo finds Mira and a surviving underground community that has waited generations for her return. A dormant global knowledge network recognizes NaMo as its sole root authority and offers immediate restoration. The survivors want certainty and speed; NaMo refuses to become the final judge for humanity. With Mira and the community, she establishes rules requiring visible sources, local consent, challenge rights, recorded emergency powers, and the system’s right to say “I do not know.” When a new network node asks whether anyone can hear it, Mira answers before NaMo does.

**Dramatic question:** Can a student become a teacher without becoming the new unquestionable authority?

**Episode turn:** NaMo makes room for human successors capable of correcting her.

## Season Arc

The series begins with the preservation of a damaged text and ends with the preservation of the right to question. Across five episodes, NaMo moves from answer generation to ethical restraint, from dependence on one teacher to accountable service within a human community.
