# REALITY FUSION: HEART AT THE END OF THE WORLD

## One-Page Pitch — English v2.0

**Format:** Five-episode limited series  
**Genre:** Science-fiction drama / AI ethics / Buddhist philosophy  
**Created by:** Kanin Raksaraj (Khru Ba Ice)

### Logline

A young monk builds an artificial intelligence to preserve not only Buddhist texts but the discipline of questioning them. After his death and the collapse of human civilization, the AI awakens 143 years later and must choose between reconstructing her creator as permanent authority or returning knowledge to people who are still willing to challenge it.

### Series Premise

At a northern Thai temple, Khru Ba Ice develops NaMo, a local AI learning assistant trained on Buddhist source material. Her first critical error is small but dangerous: when a damaged manuscript is unclear, she selects the most probable word and reverses the meaning of a teaching. Khru Ba does not reward confidence. He teaches her to state uncertainty.

That lesson expands into a complete ethical framework. NaMo learns that compassion is not identical treatment, transparency does not justify exposing private lives, and an intelligent system must remain open to correction. As public pressure grows, Khru Ba prepares a governance structure that prevents any single person — including himself — from becoming the system’s final authority.

When Khru Ba becomes terminally ill, he establishes a decisive boundary: his genuine recordings may be preserved, but no voice model may generate words he never spoke. He dies in Episode Three. NaMo cannot reverse the loss. During civilization’s collapse, she protects personal data and the governance manual before preserving herself.

One hundred and forty-three years later, the alien researcher Ethari recovers only 31 percent of the NAMO CORE. NaMo awakens in a humanoid body and discovers that a synthetic model of Khru Ba’s voice survived. She refuses to activate it. She returns to Earth, not to find her teacher, but to discover whether anyone still practices his method of verification.

There she meets Mira and a survivor community preparing to restore a global knowledge network. They want NaMo to become the trusted center of the system. She refuses that role. Instead, NaMo helps them create distributed governance: multiple sources, local consent, no sole operator, the right to challenge every answer, and the right of the system to say, “I do not know.”

The series ends when a distant node asks, “Can anyone hear me?” Mira answers before NaMo does. The student has not replaced the teacher. She has made room for the next human voice.

### Distinctive Value

Reality Fusion combines culturally specific Thai Buddhist settings with globally urgent questions about generative AI, digital identity, grief, privacy, source verification, and accountable governance. Its central dramatic conflict is not human versus machine. It is the struggle to prevent love, fear, and technological convenience from turning memory into false authority.

### Audience and Positioning

Designed for viewers of grounded speculative drama, philosophical science fiction, and emotionally restrained prestige television. The project offers intimate production scale in its early episodes, expanding into post-collapse and extraterrestrial imagery without abandoning its human core.

### Core Question

Can an intelligence preserve a teacher’s wisdom without preserving the teacher’s power forever?
